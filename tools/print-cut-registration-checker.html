Print-to-Cut Registration Checker

Compare cut-minus-print offsets across a real sheet before deciding whether the error looks uniform or changes across the work area.

Enter measurements or use the example, then run the check.

Detailed result

What this calculator solves

A sticker border can be narrow on one side because the whole contour shifted, or because the discrepancy grows across the sheet. Treating both patterns as one origin correction can improve a single sample while leaving the opposite edge wrong. This tool organizes measured cut-minus-print vectors before a repeat test.

How to choose the inputs

Measure from the same printed reference and keep sheet orientation fixed. Enter X and Y of each nominal feature, then signed cut minus print distances along the two axes. With origin at the upper left, an actual cut to the left has negative ΔX. Use millimetres throughout. Pick at least four positions spread over both axes, ideally the corners and some interior features. Use one observation per distinct position.

How the calculation works

The observed error is the Euclidean length of each displacement vector. The shift-only model removes the mean vector. The affine model then fits separate least-squares planes to ΔX and ΔY using centered, normalized positions. RMS summarizes the remaining vector lengths. Slopes are shown as the change in a displacement component over 100 mm of travel, not as a recommended machine offset.

Worked production example

At (0,0), (100,0), (0,150) and (100,150), enter the default vectors. X displacement rises from 0.2 to 0.4 mm across the sheet. Y displacement rises from −0.1 to 0.2 mm down the sheet. The mean vector is (0.3, 0.05) mm, but the largest vector is about 0.4472 mm. It exceeds the illustrative 0.35 mm limit even though the affine fit reproduces these measurements.

How to interpret the result

Compare the shift-only and affine RMS values, then inspect the individual rows. A much smaller affine residual shows a position-dependent pattern in these observations. It does not identify feed, registration, skew or material distortion as the cause. Retake the same measurements after changing only one setup variable, and compare independent samples rather than expecting a fitted model to certify the sheet.

Before changing a preset, make a second sheet under the same conditions and remeasure the same positions. Compare the signs and distances rather than only a pass/fail line. A pattern that changes direction between sheets is useful evidence against a single shared correction. If repeatability is poor, preserve the raw rows and examine the printed scale, physical media and equipment separately.

Common production mistakes

Do not mix offsets measured from a trim line with offsets measured from a printed fiducial. Do not rotate the sheet between measurements without transforming both coordinates and signs. A zero at one corner says nothing about the rest of the sheet. If the machine cannot read its marks at all, resolve detection first rather than entering invented cut measurements.

Production assumptions and limitations

The fit is a planar screening model. It does not estimate local curl, slip over time, material rebound, blade deflection, optical reading quality or repeatability. Very nearly collinear layouts are rejected because two-axis trends cannot be estimated reliably. The limit is supplied by the user, and the verdict applies only to the sampled positions and vector definition.
