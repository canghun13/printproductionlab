Print Imposition Calculator

Compare two orientations using usable sheet geometry. Values use one consistent unit (mm, in, etc.).

Enter dimensions, then calculate.

What this calculator solves

Compare two uniform layouts of one repeated rectangular item on a parent sheet. The result separates the count of pieces from the area reserved for bleed and the material that remains outside those allocations. Use it to compare candidate stock sizes before reserving paper or building the final imposition in a RIP.

The suggested orientation has the larger count within this limited comparison. It is not a mixed-orientation nesting optimizer, a PDF imposer or an automatic cutting plan. A grain requirement or finishing route can rule out that orientation even when its count is higher.

How to choose the inputs

Take parent width and height from the actual press sheet, not a finished product specification. Take finished width and height from the approved trim size. Enter bleed per edge, an outer margin on all four edges, and an additional gripper strip from the production ticket or press specification. The example values are editable planning inputs, not recommended equipment settings.

Use one linear unit throughout. The defaults represent millimetres, but consistently entered inches produce the same geometry. Do not mix a millimetre sheet with an inch trim size. Areas are the square of the input unit: mm² for millimetres and in² for inches. A zero allowance must be entered explicitly; a blank field is not a confirmed zero.

How the calculation works

Usable width = parent width − 2 × outer margin. Usable height = parent height − 2 × outer margin − additional gripper. Each item occupies a footprint equal to finished width + 2 × bleed by finished height + 2 × bleed. Across and down counts are rounded down separately, then multiplied. The rotated comparison swaps the item dimensions but does not rotate the parent sheet or move its gripper edge.

Allocated area = item count × footprint width × footprint height. Unallocated parent area = full parent area − allocated area. Its percentage uses the full parent sheet as denominator and includes outer margins, gripper and residual strips. Finished trim area instead uses the trim dimensions. Bleed allocation is not saleable trim, so those two area measures are deliberately different. A tie retains the normal orientation.

Worked production example

Use a 700 × 500 mm sheet, 90 × 50 mm finished cards, 3 mm bleed per edge, 10 mm outer margins and 12 mm additional gripper. The usable rectangle is 680 × 468 mm. Normal footprints are 96 × 56 mm: seven across and eight down give 56 pieces. Rotated footprints are 56 × 96 mm: twelve across and four down give 48.

The normal layout allocates 301,056 mm² from a 350,000 mm² parent. The unallocated 48,944 mm² is 13.98% of the parent after display rounding. Finished trim totals 252,000 mm², or 72.00%. The difference is not a second spoilage allowance; it is material included around the trim boxes. Increasing outer margin to 50 mm changes the comparison to 36 normal versus 40 rotated pieces.

How to interpret the result

Read the orientation table before using the suggested count. The scaled diagram shows the whole parent sheet, the usable rectangle, bleed boxes and finished trim. The gripper is illustrated at the bottom only to explain this geometry; confirm the actual feed edge and front/back relationship on the press layout.

Use the count as the pieces-per-sheet input in Press Sheets Required, then apply that tool's explicit sheet spoilage allowance. Compare paper cost and finishing practicality alongside area yield. This page's unallocated-area percentage is not a probability of damaged sheets and must not be entered as a spoilage rate.

Common production mistakes

Do not enter an already reduced printable area as the parent and subtract the same margins again. Do not enter a bleed-box size as the finished trim and add bleed a second time. A three-millimetre bleed on each adjoining piece allocates six millimetres between their trim edges in this model; shared bleed and extra gutters require a different production layout.

More pieces are not automatically cheaper. Grain, a clean guillotine sequence, registration marks, colour bars, folding and binding can make the lower-count orientation preferable. Do not treat the top-down schematic as a plate file, duplex instruction or safe cutter sequence.

Production assumptions and limitations

Only identical rectangular pieces in a regular grid are compared. There is no mixed nesting, independent gutter, grain constraint, work-and-turn, work-and-tumble, signature pagination, marks clearance or device imaging-window model. Gripper is additional to the four-edge outer margin; if a supplier gives a combined head allowance, reconcile that specification before entry.

Numeric overflow, underflow and counts beyond exact integer representation are rejected rather than displayed as a healthy result. Diagrams above 250 pieces retain the scaled parent and usable rectangle but omit individual pieces to bound browser rendering; this is not a machine limit. All results remain planning geometry, subject to the approved equipment and finishing route.
