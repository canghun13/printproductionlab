Roll Diameter Calculator

Estimate wound-roll diameter from material length, thickness and core diameter.

Enter values to calculate.

What this calculator solves

Roll Diameter Calculator is used during roll handling and media planning. It turns a stated job condition into a transparent planning number so an estimator, prepress operator or production coordinator can identify the next decision before committing stock, artwork or press time.

Roll Diameter Calculator is a planning tool rather than a production certificate: use its result to document a decision, then retain the source specification with the job.

How to choose the inputs

Enter roll length in metres from the supplier label or a measured rewound length. Enter total material caliper in millimetres: 100 microns is 0.10 mm, not 100 mm. For self-adhesive stock, measure the complete wound construction including face, adhesive and liner. Use the core outer diameter where winding starts, not the bore or shaft diameter.

The three defaults are an editable example, not a stock specification: 100 m length, 0.10 mm total caliper and 76 mm core OD. If dimensions arrive in inches, multiply core diameter and caliper by 25.4 before entry; convert feet to metres by multiplying by 0.3048. Roll width cancels from this uniform-thickness cross-section model and is not an input.

How the calculation works

Conserve the material area in the roll cross-section: length in mm × total caliper = π × (outside diameter² − core OD²) ÷ 4. First convert metres to millimetres by multiplying length by 1,000. Then calculate outside diameter = √(core OD² + 4 × length in m × 1,000 × caliper in mm ÷ π). The result is in millimetres.

Only length uses metres; caliper, core and output use millimetres. The square root returns a diameter, not a radius. Calculations keep full precision until the displayed result is rounded to two decimals; those decimals do not imply that a physically wound roll can be measured or guaranteed to that tolerance.

Worked production example

A 100 m roll of 0.10 mm film wound on a 76 mm OD core contains 100,000 × 0.10 = 10,000 mm² of material in cross-section. The outside diameter is √(76² + 4 × 10,000 ÷ π) = 136.05 mm. Mixing 100 metres with the two mm inputs without conversion would incorrectly give 76.08 mm, almost the bare core.

Record 100 m / 0.10 mm / 76 mm and the 136.05 mm estimated OD on the roll specification. A different job with 500 m, 0.08 mm caliper and 152.4 mm core OD gives 272.31 mm. Keep the supplier’s declared maximum OD and winding tolerance separate from either geometrical estimate.

How to interpret the result

The primary result is the estimated wound-roll outside diameter in mm. Compare that dimension with the printer’s roll-bay clearance and the supplier’s maximum delivered OD, both in mm. This calculation does not check roll mass, shaft load, core strength, usable width or brake capacity, so an OD that fits is not equipment approval.

When the estimated OD is near the machine limit, obtain a measured sample roll or a supplier winding specification before ordering the full length. Do not subtract a guessed safety margin or treat the two displayed decimals as a tolerance. Thickness variation and trapped air can make the actual roll larger.

Common production mistakes

Common unit errors are entering microns as mm, entering feet in the metre field, or using core inside diameter instead of OD. For labels, measuring only the face material understates the complete wound caliper. A radius entered as the core diameter also understates the geometry; record every measurement’s unit with the job.

The roll length is physical wound length, not the number of labels or the finished job quantity. Do not reduce that length by expected printing spoilage when checking a supplied roll’s clearance. Separate leader, trailer and splice consumption when estimating usable production yield after the roll is received.

Production assumptions and limitations

The annulus model assumes a cylindrical core, uniform total caliper and tightly wound material with no air gaps. Tissue, embossed stock, loose winding and compressible laminates may depart substantially from that model. The calculator does not select a safe winding tension or compensate for compression using an invented material factor.

Use a supplier caliper measurement taken under the specified method or measure the complete stock stack and divide by its layer count. Core wall thickness matters when converting a nominal bore to OD. Supplier winding conditions and delivered OD tolerances control release; this geometric estimate cannot replace those measurements.

Related production workflow

Use the supplier stock specification to establish length, complete caliper and core OD, then record the estimated diameter alongside the machine’s roll envelope. After delivery, measure actual OD and reconcile any discrepancy before using a separate winding-torque or job-yield calculation. Avery Dennison’s converter tools likewise distinguish core diameter, material thickness and roll length for OD planning.
