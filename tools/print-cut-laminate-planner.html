Print-and-Cut Laminate Clearance Planner

Measure the printed sheet, its artwork and its registration keep-outs. Find a rectangular laminate cut size without redrawing the cutter's marks.

Enter measurements or use the example, then run the check.

Detailed result

What this calculator solves

When the laminate is trimmed separately from the printed sheet, the operator needs an actual cut size and a placement origin. Simply subtracting a border from every edge can leave some art uncovered or can still cover an inward registration mark. This planner finds one axis-aligned rectangle covering the required artwork while avoiding the measured no-cover regions.

How to choose the inputs

Measure the physical sheet after printing, with the top-left as (0,0). Record a bounding rectangle for all art that must be laminated, including its required bleed. Record each reserved sensor area as a rectangle; use the applicable device guidance and actual mark printout rather than a guessed bounding box. Add a chosen extra clearance to account for your placement process, not a universal sensor tolerance.

How the calculation works

The tool expands each keep-out on all four sides by the entered clearance. Candidate cover edges lie at sheet boundaries or the facing edge of an expanded keep-out. For each candidate, it tests coverage of the art box and positive-area intersections with keep-outs. Among valid candidates it chooses the largest area. Boundaries may touch after clearance has been included; the optimizer does not place film outside the sheet.

Worked production example

A 210 × 297 mm sheet has reserved 20 mm strips at its top and bottom. With an illustrative additional 5 mm clearance, the allowed Y interval is 25 through 272 mm. If the required artwork is X 20 to 190 and Y 35 to 255, the largest candidate is 210 × 247 mm at (0,25). Moving the art start to Y 21 puts it inside the expanded top keep-out and returns no solution.

How to interpret the result

Use the placement coordinates to make a physical trial and check that the piece fits your actual film stock and application process. A geometric fit cannot tell whether the sensor will read, whether the film is compatible, or whether its gloss, lighting or adhesion will cause a problem. On a new stock, verify a single real print before committing the rest of the job.

Make a small trial with the same film and application method. Mark the intended origin on a removable scrap guide rather than on the machine fiducial. Measure the applied position after smoothing the film; an entry error in either top-left coordinate can move the clear window even when the cut rectangle has the right width and height.

Common production mistakes

Do not measure the artwork and marks from differently scaled previews. Do not use this output as a registration-mark generator, and do not move machine marks merely to clear an intersection. The art box should represent required coverage, not the full sheet unless all of it really needs protection. Curved or segmented laminate cannot be represented by this one-rectangle model.

Production assumptions and limitations

All geometry is rectangular and measured in millimetres. The tool does not prescribe printer models, optical mark shapes, temperature, pressure, compatible film or a sensor scan path. A no-fit result is a job-planning decision, not an equipment error. If a model uses a large exclusion region, enter that entire region or a conservative rectangular envelope.
