Post-Press Time & Cost Planner

Transparent post-press planning with editable job assumptions.

Enter values to calculate.

What this calculator solves

Estimate cutting, folding, lamination, creasing, binding or a user-defined finishing step using transparent hourly inputs. It makes the job assumptions visible instead of treating a generic production rule as a guaranteed specification.

Use the result as a job-ticket planning value. A folding dummy, measured stock and the selected equipment remain the controlling evidence.

When to use it and how to choose inputs

Use Post-Press Time & Cost Planner after trim size and production sequence are known, but before artwork is released or materials are ordered. Enter values in one unit system and choose the allowance, rate and costs from the actual job ticket; editable example values are not shop standards.

Metric and imperial dimensions are both accepted when consistently entered. Zero, negative, missing and non-finite values are rejected because they cannot produce a useful production plan.

Calculation method

Run hours equal quantity × passes ÷ rate. Total time adds setup; labour cost multiplies total hours by operators and labour rate, then machine and material costs are added.

The calculation shows its arithmetic rather than claiming to certify machine setup. Round sheet, roll and lift counts up only where a whole physical unit is required.

Worked production example

A 5,000-piece two-pass operation at 1,500/hour with 45 minutes setup has 7.42 total hours before downstream waiting or cure time.

Record the inputs with the job: a later change in stock, sequence, make-ready or process speed should trigger a fresh estimate.

Interpretation and common mistakes

Keep separate rows or rerun for each process; a single blended speed hides bottlenecks.

Compare the result with the approved job specification and test a dummy where a fold, coating or material behaviour can change the outcome.

Assumptions and limitations

This is an estimating and preflight aid, not a production-certified value. Stock caliper, grain, coating, cure, static, curl, blade condition, fold direction and equipment setup can materially change results.
