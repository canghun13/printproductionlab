Folding Allowance Planner

Transparent post-press planning with editable job assumptions.

Enter values to calculate.

What this calculator solves

Turn an agreed allowance and panel count into a documented preflight schedule rather than inventing a universal compensation. It makes the job assumptions visible instead of treating a generic production rule as a guaranteed specification.

Folding Allowance Planner is a job-ticket planning value: the folding dummy, measured stock and selected equipment remain the controlling evidence.

When to use it and how to choose inputs

Use Folding Allowance Planner after trim size and production sequence are known, but before artwork is released or materials are ordered. Enter values in one unit system and choose the allowance, rate and costs from the actual job ticket; editable example values are not shop standards.

For Folding Allowance Planner, metric and imperial dimensions work when consistently entered. Zero, negative, missing and non-finite values are rejected because they cannot produce a useful production plan.

Calculation method

Flat width is panel count × base panel width minus the triangular cumulative reduction: allowance × n × (n − 1) ÷ 2.

Folding Allowance Planner shows its arithmetic rather than claiming to certify machine setup. Round sheet, roll and lift counts up only where a whole physical unit is required.

Worked production example

Four 4 in panels with 1/16 in reductions need 15.625 in of flat width.

Record Folding Allowance Planner inputs with the job: a later change in stock, sequence, make-ready or process speed should trigger a fresh estimate.

Interpretation and common mistakes

Do not treat caliper as an automatic allowance; use it only alongside a dummy and vendor direction.

Compare the Folding Allowance Planner result with the approved job specification and test a dummy where a fold, coating or material behaviour can change the outcome.

Assumptions and limitations

Folding Allowance Planner is an estimating and preflight aid, not a production-certified value. Stock caliper, grain, coating, cure, static, curl, blade condition, fold direction and equipment setup can materially change results.
