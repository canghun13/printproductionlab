GSM and Basis Weight Converter

Convert a stated basis weight only after identifying the stock's basis sheet size.

Enter values.

Paper grades use different basis sizes; this tool intentionally requires dimensions rather than guessing a grade.

What this calculator solves

GSM and Basis Weight Converter is used during paper stock comparison and purchasing. It turns a stated job condition into a transparent planning number so an estimator, prepress operator or production coordinator can identify the next decision before committing stock, artwork or press time.

GSM and Basis Weight Converter is a planning tool rather than a production certificate: use its result to document a decision, then retain the source specification with the job.

How to choose the inputs

Enter the supplier’s basis weight in pounds and the stated basis-sheet width and height in inches. Basis weight is not comparable without its grade and basis size; GSM expresses mass per square metre.

The fields on this GSM and Basis Weight Converter page are: Basis weight (lb), Basis width (in), Basis height (in). Read the label’s unit literally and convert source measurements before entry. A numeric result is meaningful only when every input refers to the same finished piece, press sheet, roll length or production quantity.

How the calculation works

GSM and Basis Weight Converter uses the entered values in the displayed order, preserves the calculation precision internally and rounds the displayed planning value. Where a result represents a count of sheets, jobs or layouts, round up only after deciding which quantity must be whole.

Check the GSM and Basis Weight Converter relationship against the job ticket: the arithmetic can be exact while the selected production basis is wrong.

Worked production example

A 70 lb stock on a 25 by 38 inch basis sheet converts to about 104 gsm. Record both the grade and basis size on the estimate so a similarly named stock is not substituted.

Record the GSM and Basis Weight Converter inputs and result on the estimate or production ticket. That lets a later operator trace whether a change came from quantity, stock, layout or a changed process allowance.

How to interpret the result

GSM and Basis Weight Converter provides a planning value, not an automatic release instruction. Apply its result to the relevant quote, material order, artwork brief, press plan, finishing schedule or inventory reservation, then compare it with the approved job specification.

If the GSM and Basis Weight Converter value sits close to a sheet, roll or capacity limit, rerun the calculation with the actual machine and supplier conditions before committing.

Common production mistakes

A frequent GSM and Basis Weight Converter error is converting pounds without basis dimensions or assuming bond, text and cover grades share one basis size. Also confirm that quantity means saleable pieces, sheets, impressions or linear length as labelled; those measures are not interchangeable.

For GSM and Basis Weight Converter, keep spoilage, makeready, overage and finishing loss visible as separate assumptions so they are not counted twice.

Production assumptions and limitations

Mill specifications and paper category govern the correct basis sheet; verify the supplier data sheet before purchase. GSM and Basis Weight Converter does not certify equipment fit, final colour, binding performance or delivered quality.

Before releasing a GSM and Basis Weight Converter decision, confirm the press, stock, RIP, drying, coating, binding and finishing conditions that apply to this job rather than relying on a universal default.

Related production workflow

Start with the job ticket and approved artwork or stock specification, use this calculation at the paper stock comparison and purchasing stage, then carry the recorded result into the next estimating, prepress or production decision.
